What margin is a 125% markup? 55.56% (price = cost × 2.25)

Last reviewed: 2026-10-30

Short answer: a 125% markup on cost gives a 55.56% profit margin on the selling price. Multiply cost by 2.25 to get the price; 55.56% of that price is gross profit.

The formula

Markup is profit as a share of cost; margin is profit as a share of selling price.

  • Price = cost × (1 + markup) = cost × 2.25
  • Margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup) = 1.25 ÷ 2.25 = 55.56%
  • Reverse: markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin)

Worked example in SAR and USD

SAR: cost 80 SAR × 2.25 = 180.00 SAR price (excluding VAT). Profit = 100.00 SAR, which is 55.56% of 180.00. With Saudi 15% VAT added on top, the customer pays 207.00 SAR; the 27.00 SAR VAT is not yours.

USD: cost $25 × 2.25 = $56.25. Profit = $31.25, a 55.56% margin. Add sales tax or VAT according to where you sell.

Reverse: selling price for a target margin

If you start from the margin instead, price = cost ÷ (1 − margin). For the 55.56% margin this markup gives: cost ÷ (1 − 55.56%) = cost × 2.25, the same price. Starting from a VAT-inclusive shelf price, the highest cost that still keeps a 125% markup is shelf price ÷ 1.15 ÷ 2.25:

Can the margin be 125%?

No. Margin is profit ÷ selling price, so it is always below 100% while cost is above zero. A 125% markup is a 55.56% margin; even a 1,000% markup is only a 90.91% margin.

Common mistake: markup on a VAT-inclusive price

If you set the VAT-inclusive price at cost × 2.25, 15% VAT comes out of it, which means you really earn a 95.65% markup (48.89% margin), not 125%. Apply the markup to the price excluding VAT, then add VAT.

Gross margin is not net profit

This margin is before payment fees, shipping, cash-on-delivery failures, returns and ads. Meezan's free calculator works out your real net profit per order from your Salla, Zid or Shopify export.
