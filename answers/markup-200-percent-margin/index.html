What margin is a 200% markup? 66.67% (price = cost × 3.00)

Last reviewed: 2026-10-30

Short answer: a 200% markup on cost gives a 66.67% profit margin on the selling price. Multiply cost by 3.00 to get the price; 66.67% of that price is gross profit.

The formula

Markup is profit as a share of cost; margin is profit as a share of selling price.

  • Price = cost × (1 + markup) = cost × 3.00
  • Margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup) = 2.00 ÷ 3.00 = 66.67%
  • Reverse: markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin)

Worked example in SAR and USD

SAR: cost 80 SAR × 3.00 = 240.00 SAR price (excluding VAT). Profit = 160.00 SAR, which is 66.67% of 240.00. With Saudi 15% VAT added on top, the customer pays 276.00 SAR; the 36.00 SAR VAT is not yours.

USD: cost $25 × 3.00 = $75.00. Profit = $50.00, a 66.67% margin. Add sales tax or VAT according to where you sell.

Reverse: selling price for a target margin

If you start from the margin instead, price = cost ÷ (1 − margin). For the 66.67% margin this markup gives: cost ÷ (1 − 66.67%) = cost × 3.00, the same price. Starting from a VAT-inclusive shelf price, the highest cost that still keeps a 200% markup is shelf price ÷ 1.15 ÷ 3.00:

Can the margin be 200%?

No. Margin is profit ÷ selling price, so it is always below 100% while cost is above zero. A 200% markup is a 66.67% margin; even a 1,000% markup is only a 90.91% margin.

Common mistake: markup on a VAT-inclusive price

If you set the VAT-inclusive price at cost × 3.00, 15% VAT comes out of it, which means you really earn a 160.87% markup (61.67% margin), not 200%. Apply the markup to the price excluding VAT, then add VAT.

Gross margin is not net profit

This margin is before payment fees, shipping, cash-on-delivery failures, returns and ads. Meezan's free calculator works out your real net profit per order from your Salla, Zid or Shopify export.
