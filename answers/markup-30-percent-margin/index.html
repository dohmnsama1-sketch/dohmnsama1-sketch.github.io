What margin is a 30% markup? 23.08% (price = cost × 1.30)

Last reviewed: 2026-10-30

Short answer: a 30% markup on cost gives a 23.08% profit margin on the selling price. Multiply cost by 1.30 to get the price; 23.08% of that price is gross profit.

The formula

Markup is profit as a share of cost; margin is profit as a share of selling price.

  • Price = cost × (1 + markup) = cost × 1.30
  • Margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup) = 0.30 ÷ 1.30 = 23.08%
  • Reverse: markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin)

Worked example in SAR and USD

SAR: cost 80 SAR × 1.30 = 104.00 SAR price (excluding VAT). Profit = 24.00 SAR, which is 23.08% of 104.00. With Saudi 15% VAT added on top, the customer pays 119.60 SAR; the 15.60 SAR VAT is not yours.

USD: cost $25 × 1.30 = $32.50. Profit = $7.50, a 23.08% margin. Add sales tax or VAT according to where you sell.

Reverse: selling price for a target margin

If you start from the margin instead, price = cost ÷ (1 − margin). For the 23.08% margin this markup gives: cost ÷ (1 − 23.08%) = cost × 1.30, the same price. Starting from a VAT-inclusive shelf price, the highest cost that still keeps a 30% markup is shelf price ÷ 1.15 ÷ 1.30:

Did you actually want a 30% margin?

Markup and margin are often mixed up. A 30% margin means profit is 30% of the selling price, so price = cost ÷ (1 − 0.30) ≈ cost × 1.4286. That needs a markup of 42.86%, not 30%. For an 80 SAR cost the price would be 114.29 SAR (vs 104.00 SAR with a 30% markup).

Common mistake: markup on a VAT-inclusive price

If you set the VAT-inclusive price at cost × 1.30, 15% VAT comes out of it, which means you really earn a 13.04% markup (11.54% margin), not 30%. Apply the markup to the price excluding VAT, then add VAT.

Gross margin is not net profit

This margin is before payment fees, shipping, cash-on-delivery failures, returns and ads. Meezan's free calculator works out your real net profit per order from your Salla, Zid or Shopify export.
