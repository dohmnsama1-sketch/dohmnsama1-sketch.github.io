What margin is a 90% markup? 47.37% (price = cost × 1.90)

Last reviewed: 2026-10-30

Short answer: a 90% markup on cost gives a 47.37% profit margin on the selling price. Multiply cost by 1.90 to get the price; 47.37% of that price is gross profit.

The formula

Markup is profit as a share of cost; margin is profit as a share of selling price.

  • Price = cost × (1 + markup) = cost × 1.90
  • Margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup) = 0.90 ÷ 1.90 = 47.37%
  • Reverse: markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin)

Worked example in SAR and USD

SAR: cost 80 SAR × 1.90 = 152.00 SAR price (excluding VAT). Profit = 72.00 SAR, which is 47.37% of 152.00. With Saudi 15% VAT added on top, the customer pays 174.80 SAR; the 22.80 SAR VAT is not yours.

USD: cost $25 × 1.90 = $47.50. Profit = $22.50, a 47.37% margin. Add sales tax or VAT according to where you sell.

Reverse: selling price for a target margin

If you start from the margin instead, price = cost ÷ (1 − margin). For the 47.37% margin this markup gives: cost ÷ (1 − 47.37%) = cost × 1.90, the same price. Starting from a VAT-inclusive shelf price, the highest cost that still keeps a 90% markup is shelf price ÷ 1.15 ÷ 1.90:

Did you actually want a 90% margin?

Markup and margin are often mixed up. A 90% margin means profit is 90% of the selling price, so price = cost ÷ (1 − 0.90) = cost × 10.00. That needs a markup of 900%, not 90%. For an 80 SAR cost the price would be 800.00 SAR (vs 152.00 SAR with a 90% markup).

Common mistake: markup on a VAT-inclusive price

If you set the VAT-inclusive price at cost × 1.90, 15% VAT comes out of it, which means you really earn a 65.22% markup (39.47% margin), not 90%. Apply the markup to the price excluding VAT, then add VAT.

Gross margin is not net profit
