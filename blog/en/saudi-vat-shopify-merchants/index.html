Saudi VAT (15%) for Shopify Merchants: Registration, Pricing, Returns

Last reviewed: 2026-10-28 · 4 min read

If you run a Shopify store that sells to customers in Saudi Arabia, VAT affects three things: whether you must register, how you show prices, and how you calculate profit. This guide summarises what ZATCA's published e-commerce guideline says, shows the arithmetic, and flags what to confirm with an accountant. It is general information, not tax advice, and rules change, so check ZATCA before you act.

Do you need to register?

Summarising the VAT Implementing Regulations as reported by tax guides:

  • Mandatory registration when your taxable supplies exceed SAR 375,000 over the previous 12 months, or you expect them to in the next 12 months. You must apply within 30 days of the end of the month in which the threshold was exceeded or expected to be exceeded.
  • Voluntary registration is available when taxable supplies or expenses exceed SAR 187,500 but stay below the mandatory limit.

The thresholds are measured on taxable supplies, not profit. See ZATCA's Implementing Regulations and their SME guidance for current wording. Registration itself, and any identity or tax details, are entered by you on ZATCA's portal.

Prices shown to consumers include VAT

ZATCA's Guideline on Electronic Commerce under VAT Provisions (section 4.2.3) says the final prices advertised to consumers on websites, platforms, apps and social-media stores include VAT, and that VAT is calculated as 15/115 of the inclusive price. Separately, the E-Commerce Law (Article 7) requires the total price including fees, taxes and delivery amounts to be disclosed.

Inclusive price (SAR) VAT (15/115) Revenue before VAT
100 13.04 86.96
230 30.00 200.00
1,000 130.43 869.57

A common mistake is to take 15% of the inclusive price (34.50 on 230) instead of 15/115 (30.00).

Shopify settings

Shopify's help documents describe how to show tax-inclusive prices (Include taxes in product prices). Its dynamic tax-inclusive pricing page lists Saudi Arabia (and the UAE and Qatar, among others) as markets where, once you have set up tax collection and activated the feature, the local rate is automatically included in storefront prices. Always test a checkout in your own store before relying on a setting.

What VAT means for profit

If you are VAT-registered, the VAT you collect is not revenue; it is owed to ZATCA after input-tax deductions. Calculate profit from revenue before VAT. On a SAR 230 product: revenue is 200, not 230. If you are not registered, you are not collecting VAT and your revenue is simply the price you charge. Either way, know which case you are in, because your profit maths differs.

Gateway fees, shipping and ads often carry their own VAT. If you are registered you may be able to recover that as input tax, with a valid tax invoice and a link to your taxable activity (see ZATCA's input-tax guidance). If you are not registered it is a cost to you.

Returns need a credit note

ZATCA's guideline says that when goods are returned to the domestic supplier and the supply is cancelled, the supplier must adjust the output tax and issue a credit note. A refund is therefore both a payment and a tax document. Ask your accountant how credit notes are issued from your setup.

Free gifts, giveaways and "free delivery"

Section 4.2.4 of the guideline covers "presumed supplies": goods given away without consideration can be treated as a taxable supply, with exceptions. One exception covers promotional gifts and samples if the market value per recipient does not exceed SAR 200 per calendar year and total such supplies do not exceed SAR 50,000 per calendar year. ZATCA's own examples say a free item bundled with a paid purchase is not a separate supply, while a prize given to a draw winner may be subject to VAT unless an exception applies. "Free delivery" bundled into a larger sale is generally not a separate free supply, but for your own profit it is still a cost you pay the carrier.

Put it into a profit calculation

Example (illustrative): price 230, product cost 70, carrier 22, packaging 3, mada fee 5.03 (1.75% + SAR 1 assumed). Revenue 200. Profit before ads = 200 − 70 − 22 − 3 − 5.03 = SAR 99.97. With a SAR 35 ad cost per order, profit is SAR 64.97.

FAQ

Should I add 15% on top of my price? If you are registered, the price shown to consumers must already include VAT. If you want to keep SAR 200 after VAT, show SAR 230.

Do I need e-invoicing? ZATCA rolls out e-invoicing in phases and waves. Check ZATCA's e-invoicing pages for your obligations.

Does the 375,000 threshold use profit? No, it uses taxable supplies (sales), not profit.
