How to Calculate Real Net Profit for a Shopify Store in Saudi Arabia

Last reviewed: 2026-10-28 · 4 min read

Shopify's dashboard shows sales. Your bank account shows something smaller. Between the two sit VAT, payment fees, shipping, cash-on-delivery charges, returns and ad spend. If you sell to Saudi Arabia or elsewhere in the GCC, a few local details make the gap larger than most merchants expect. This guide walks through the calculation on an illustrative example, then shows how to run it on your real Shopify export.

Step 1: Take VAT out of the price

In Saudi Arabia, prices shown to consumers are expected to be VAT-inclusive. ZATCA's Guideline on Electronic Commerce under VAT Provisions (section 4.2.3) says final prices advertised to consumers include VAT, and that the tax is 15/115 of the VAT-inclusive price. In Shopify you can show tax-inclusive prices through your tax settings; Shopify's help page on including taxes in prices explains the options for your market.

Example (all numbers illustrative): a product sells for SAR 230 including VAT.

  • VAT = 230 × 15 ÷ 115 = SAR 30. That money belongs to the tax authority if you are registered.
  • Revenue = 230 − 30 = SAR 200.

If you calculate profit from 230 instead of 200, you overstate profit by 15% on day one. (Other GCC countries have different VAT rates, and some have none. Check your own market.)

Step 2: Subtract the direct cost of each order

Assume these illustrative per-order costs:

Item SAR
Product cost 70
Shipping you pay the carrier 22
Packaging 3
Card payment fee (mada at 1.75% + SAR 1, assumed) 5.03

Profit before ads = 200 − 70 − 22 − 3 − 5.03 = SAR 99.97 per order. This is your contribution: what each order leaves to pay for ads and overheads.

Step 3: Subtract the cost of getting the customer

If you pay SAR 35 in ads for this order, profit is 99.97 − 35 = SAR 64.97. The naive view, price minus product cost, says 230 − 70 = 160. The difference between 160 and 65 is exactly what dashboards hide.

Fixed monthly costs (apps, salaries, rent, design) come off the monthly total, not each order.

Step 4: Count the leaks

  1. Payment fees differ by method. Gateway fees are usually a percentage plus a fixed amount, so small orders pay a higher effective rate. Mada, cards, Apple Pay, Tabby and Tamara each cost different amounts. Tabby's pricing page says the commission varies by merchant and is set in your contract; it does not publish a universal rate, so use your own contract numbers.
  2. Non-Shopify-Payments providers can add a platform fee. Shopify's help pages state that a third-party transaction fee can apply when you use a payment provider other than Shopify Payments, on top of the provider's own fees. Check your plan's rate. Shopify's supported countries page lists the United Arab Emirates for Shopify Payments but does not list Saudi Arabia, so many Saudi merchants rely on third-party gateways.
  3. Cash on delivery. Carriers charge a collection fee, and refused orders cost you outbound and return shipping. Track your real refusal rate; do not borrow someone else's.
  4. Returns. Saudi Arabia's E-Commerce Law (Article 13) gives consumers a right to withdraw within seven days of receiving a product, with listed exceptions, and says the consumer bears withdrawal costs unless the parties agree otherwise (law text). If you offer free returns, the cost is yours. For VAT-registered sellers, returned goods require an output-tax adjustment and a credit note per ZATCA's guideline.
  5. Free shipping and discounts. Free shipping is a cost you pay. Discounts reduce revenue; check that your export captures them.

In our example, if 10% of orders are returned (goods come back resellable, gateway fee not refunded), average profit before ads falls from SAR 99.97 to about SAR 84.78 per order.

Step 5: Use your real Shopify data

Shopify lets you export orders as CSV from the Orders page; larger exports are emailed to you (Exporting orders, the export CSV includes fields like Subtotal, Shipping, Taxes, Total, Discount Amount and Financial Status). Export a recent month and calculate by payment method rather than using one blended number.

Quick checklist

  • Revenue = VAT-inclusive price ÷ 1.15 (Saudi Arabia).
  • Subtract product cost, shipping, packaging and payment fees to get profit before ads.
  • Subtract ad cost per order.
  • Include returns, free shipping and COD refusals.
  • Re-run monthly on the actual export.
