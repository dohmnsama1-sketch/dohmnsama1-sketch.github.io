The problem

A passing score is only as trustworthy as the verifier.

If a grader accepts malformed rows, duplicate keys, type mismatches or incorrect values, your benchmark can misrepresent system performance. These checks are most useful when the correct response is strictly specified and machine-verifiable.

01 / Input contract

Explicit reference semantics

Define supported row keys, columns, scalar types and exact target values. The pilot begins with an agreed reference contract—not a vague correctness claim.

02 / Mutation pack

Reproducible edge probes

Generate valid controls alongside invalid variants to test strict parser behavior, schema drift, missing entries and wrong-value acceptance.

03 / Audit output

Traceable disagreement reports

Compare expected outcomes to the grader decisions you run. Export case-level JSON/HTML findings with missing-evidence counts and reproducible IDs.

Workflow

Three steps. Clear boundaries.

Agree the contract

Provide a synthetic or otherwise appropriately permitted tabular JSON reference, validation expectations and your grader's documented input/output format.

Run the probes

We prepare a finite probe pack. You execute it against your grader in your environment and send back the booleans and probe IDs—not production access.

Reconcile and rerun

Receive a mismatch report and suggested checks; one agreed rerun is included after you adjust your grader.

Questions

Fit before commitment

Is this a SaaS subscription or a paid service?

Currently a bounded consulting/QA pilot supported by a local prototype, not a self-serve subscription. No live production SLA or subscription is offered.

Is the grader tested on your infrastructure?

No. The buyer runs the probes against its grader and returns the result map. This helps avoid collecting credentials or private code.

Can you support arbitrary output formats?

No. The current pilot is restricted to supported exact-output tabular JSON contracts and scalar types. Other formats require separate scoping and feasibility checks.

What is the sample's reported 17%?

The sample has 17 false-positive acceptances out of 30 probes (not 17%). They were deliberately produced by a weak bundled synthetic grader; they do not represent production error rates.
